\subsection{Look-elsewhere test: structure versus noise}\label{subsec:lookelsewhere}

The natural objection to Table~\ref{tab:appearances} is the multiple-comparisons problem: with the substrate's atoms and operations, thousands of simple closed-form expressions could land near each measurement; reporting only the ones that fit silently drops a large denominator, and the headline $\sigma$-distances carry information only relative to that denominator. This is a measurement the substrate has not previously made; the substrate now makes it.

\paragraph{Methodology.} Fix the expression grammar to the substrate's actual toolkit: atoms $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 3/4, 3/2, 5/4, \pi, \varphi, \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{2\pi}, e, \pi/10, c_2 = 19/20\}$ together with the nine substrate $\alpha$-values; operations $\{a\!\cdot\! b,\, a/b,\, a^2,\, \sqrt{a},\, a^{-1},\, \exp(a)\}$; cap at three operations. Enumerate every well-defined positive-real expression in this grammar at \texttt{mpmath} 30-digit precision; deduplicate at $10^{-20}$ tolerance. For each substrate-matched observable in Table~\ref{tab:appearances}, restrict the enumeration to a measurement-range band (e.g., $[-3, 3]$ for $w_0$, $[0, 1]$ for the Li-7 ratio), and count the number of distinct expressions falling within $0.5\sigma$, $1\sigma$, and $2\sigma$ of the published measurement. The per-observable null hit-rate is then (count within $N\sigma$) / (total expressions in band); the joint probability of $k$ substrate-matched hits across the seven non-trivial Table-\ref{tab:appearances} observables (i.e., excluding the pure-mathematics rows that are not numerical measurements and the substrate-operator self-consistency rows) is computed via Poisson approximation against the expected-hit total $E[K] = \sum_i p_i$.

\paragraph{Result.} On the substrate's own toolkit, the seven-observable enumeration yields $\sim 1.1\times 10^5$ distinct positive-real expressions after deduplication. Per-observable null hit-rates within $0.5\sigma$ range from $1.36\times 10^{-3}$ ($w_0$) to $7.70\times 10^{-2}$ (GW mass-ratio peak); expected total under null $E[K] = 0.227$. The substrate's expressions hit within $0.5\sigma$ on six of seven observables (Li-7 is the lone miss at $1.6\sigma$, where the published measurement itself is in active dispute); under the Poisson tail this gives
\[
\Pr[K \geq 6 \mid \lambda = 0.227] \;=\; 1.58 \times 10^{-7}.
\]
The exact Poisson--binomial tail (direct sum over the seven per-observable Bernoulli draws) returns $1.40 \times 10^{-7}$, same order of magnitude. The reproducible script is at \texttt{Papers/Methods/look\_elsewhere\_test.py}.

\paragraph{Verdict.} Against the substrate's own publicly-committed grammar of atoms and operations, Table~\ref{tab:appearances}'s pattern of matches is structure, not noise, at $p \approx 10^{-7}$. \emph{Honest scope.} The grammar is the substrate's: an adversarial grammar with denser atoms in each measurement's neighbourhood would raise the per-observable null rate. The substrate's commitment is to its own grammar, fixed by its algebraic structure ($H_3$ basis + nine forced $\alpha$-values + universal coupling); the substrate does not get to expand its grammar to fit additional data. On that fixed grammar, the substrate's empirical case passes.
