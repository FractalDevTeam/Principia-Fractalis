## 0. What a substrate-to-observable map must be

In algebraic quantum theory a physical model is a triple (A, ω, dynamics): a
C*-algebra A of observables, a state ω giving expectation values ω(O) for
self-adjoint O ∈ A, and a one-parameter automorphism group for time evolution. A
*measured number* is ω(O) or a spectral datum of a chosen generator.

The substrate fixes the first two slots and almost nothing else:
- **A = T_∞**, the UHF 3^∞ C*-algebra — **PROVEN** forced/unique (`T_infinity_rigidity`,
  kernel-clean, public at commit 398e3dfd, tag `substrate-rigidity-v1`).
- **ω = τ**, the **unique** continuous unital tracial linear functional — **PROVEN**
  (`Substrate3Inf.trace_unique`, kernel-clean). The substrate does not merely admit a
  trace; it admits exactly one.
- **dynamics**: the substrate supplies **none**. The modular group of a trace is
  trivial. Time evolution is NOT determined by (A, τ). This is the first place the map
  must inject structure not present in the substrate.

The intrinsic outputs split into two channels, and — a correction forced by the
rigidity theorem itself — they live at **different levels**:

1. **Algebra channel (trace values).** τ(O), and in particular τ(p) for projections p.
   Genuine invariants of the C*-algebra T_∞; their range is ℤ[1/3] (§1).
2. **Presentation channel (base-3 abscissa).** σ(α) = log₃|1 + 2cos(πα)|, **derived**:
   from the block identity Σ_{n<3^N} z^{D₃(n)} = (1 + z + z²)^N
   (`TuringEncoding/DigitalSum.lean:148`), at z = e^{iπα} the block modulus is
   |1 + 2cos(πα)|, so block sums grow like 3^{Nσ(α)} and σ is the abscissa of
   convergence of R_f(α,s) = Σ e^{iπα D₃(n)}/n^s. Nothing fitted. **But σ is a property
   of the base-3 DIGIT STRUCTURE, not of the algebra:** `T_infinity_rigidity` proves the
   C*-algebra forgets its digit labels (it is fixed up to ∗-isomorphism by the bare
   tower), whereas D₃ lives on those labels. So σ is not "the substrate's output" — it
   is the ternary **presentation's** output. This is the closed K-channel (§1) seen from
   the other side: the algebra's invariants cannot carry an irrational, and σ carries one
   *precisely because it is not an algebra invariant*. A map through σ therefore takes the
   base-3 presentation as input, which is itself a posit.

Everything a map can honestly say about physics passes through one of these two
channels, or declares the extra input it adds.
