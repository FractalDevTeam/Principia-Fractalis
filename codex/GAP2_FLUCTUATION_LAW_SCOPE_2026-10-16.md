## 1. What "derive the fluctuation law" means, precisely

The law is the **second-order structure of the matter process**. For a
position process `X` on a manifold `(M,g)` with generator `L`, the law
`<dxi^2> = 2D dt` is any of these equivalent statements:

* quadratic variation: `[X^mu, X^nu]_t = 2D g^{mu nu} t`
* generator principal part: `L = D Delta_g + b.grad`
* carre du champ: `Gamma(f,f) = 2D |grad f|^2_g`
* Ito coefficient: `a^{mu nu} = 2D g^{mu nu}`

The `+-` is **Nelson's two-sided calculus**, not an ambiguity. With forward /
backward mean derivatives
`D_+ f = lim_{h->0+} E[(f(X_{t+h})-f(X_t))/h | F_t]` and the time-reversed
`D_-`, a diffusion with coefficient `2D` has
`D_+ = d_t + b.grad + D Delta` and `D_- = d_t + b_*.grad - D Delta`.
Forward conditioning gives `+2D dt`, backward gives `-2D dt`.

**So "derive it from a geometry<->matter field equation" decomposes into four
separable conclusions.** This decomposition is the main output of this scope:

> Let `(M,g)` be Lorentzian, let the matter content be a Markov process `X`
> on `M` (equivalently a measure on path space), and suppose a covariant field
> equation `E[g, X] = 0` holds. Conclude:
> **(i)** `X` is a diffusion — continuous paths, finite nonzero quadratic
> variation, no jump part;
> **(ii)** its diffusion tensor is conformal to the metric,
> `a^{mu nu} = 2D g^{mu nu}`;
> **(iii)** the conformal factor `D` is constant, not a scalar field;
> **(iv)** `D = hbar/2m`.

(i)-(iii) are one mathematical problem. (iv) is a different kind of claim and
is separately obstructed — see section 4.

## 2. What already exists

**Nelson (1966, 1985) — gives the CONSEQUENCE, not the law.** Given (i)-(iv)
plus the stochastic Newton law `m a = -grad V` with
`a = (D_+ D_- + D_- D_+)/2`, one derives the Madelung equations and hence
Schrodinger. Yasue's stochastic variational principle tightens this to a
genuine least-action formulation. **Nelson postulates the fluctuation law; he
does not derive it.** That is exactly gap 2.

**Lyons' rough path theory — the sharpest asset, and it is a negative.** A
rough path is a *pair* `(X, XX)`: the path plus its second level (iterated
integral / Levy area). **The second level is not determined by `X`** — the Ito
and Stratonovich lifts of the same Brownian path are different rough paths
with different brackets. So the fluctuation law is **provably independent
data**, not something readable off a trajectory. Two payoffs: it resolves gap
1 (a rigorous calculus for nondifferentiable paths), and it *types* the
missing object in gap 2 — a symmetric 2-tensor-valued path, which the
"conformal to `g`" condition (ii) reduces to a single scalar `D`.

**Eells-Elworthy-Malliavin — gives (ii), conditionally.** Brownian motion on a
Riemannian manifold is constructed as the diffusion generated by
`(1/2)Delta_g` via stochastic development on the orthonormal frame bundle.
So **if** one posits that the matter process is the metric-canonical
diffusion, (ii) is automatic. Bakry-Emery `Gamma`-calculus states the same
content as `Gamma(f,f) = |grad f|^2_g`. The work in (ii) is therefore not
"prove the tensor is conformal to `g`" but "derive that the process is the
metric-canonical one rather than assume it."

**Franchi-Le Jan — covariant relativistic diffusion.** A genuinely covariant
diffusion on the frame bundle of a Lorentzian manifold. Note for scoping: the
**diffusivity is an extra parameter**, not determined by `g`. Same gap.

**Bakry-Emery curvature-dimension — closest existing geometry<->fluctuation
link.** `Gamma_2(f) >= (1/n)(Lf)^2 + K Gamma(f)` ties Ricci curvature to the
diffusion's second-order behaviour. But it *constrains a given diffusion*; it
does not produce the coefficient.

**mathlib at this pin (`eed770a4`):** present — martingales (10 files),
stopping times (7), conditional expectation (24), Markov kernels,

**Neither side of the gap-2 equation is statable in Lean today.** This is not

## 3. What is genuinely missing — and the obstruction to lead with

**The averaging obstruction.** An Einstein-type field equation is an equation
in `T_{mu nu}`. For a matter process, `T_{mu nu}` depends only on low-order
moments of the *marginal* distributions — density, current, pressure. The
fluctuation law is **path-level** data. The map
`{measures on path space} -> {T_{mu nu}}` is many-to-one: a diffusion and a
pure-jump process can share `rho` and `j`, hence share `T_{mu nu}`.

**Therefore no field equation written in `T_{mu nu}` alone can fix the
fluctuation law.** Conclusion (i) in particular — "no jump part" — is
invisible to the stress tensor.

This determines the *shape* the sought equation must have: **the unknown must
be the law on path space, not the stress tensor.** That means a variational or
Gibbs principle on path space, not a PDE in `T_{mu nu}`. Yasue's principle is
of exactly that type — and it takes `D` as input.

So the exact open problem:

> **Open problem (gap 2 core).** Exhibit a covariant variational principle on
> path-space measures over a Lorentzian manifold whose stationary points
> necessarily (a) are diffusions, (b) have diffusion tensor conformal to `g`,
> and (c) with constant conformal factor.

Sub-structure: (a) is a regularity theorem for the matter sector — the
hardest and least-studied part. (b) looks reachable given EEM +
`Gamma`-calculus, but the principle must *select* the metric-canonical
diffusion rather than assume it. (c) needs a rigidity argument ruling out
`D` as a nonconstant scalar field.

## 4. Can `D = hbar/2m` be separated from the derivation?

**Yes — cleanly, but in the unhelpful direction. (i)-(iii) can be attacked
with no `hbar` anywhere; (iv) provably cannot come from a geometry<->matter
coupling built on `G` and `c`.**

`[D] = L^2 T^-1`. From `G`, `c`, and the particle mass `m`, the only such
combination is `G m / c` (dimensionless factors aside). Compare:

| | scaling | electron | proton |
|---|---|---|---|
| `D_quantum = hbar/2m` | `~ m^-1` | `5.78838e-5` m^2/s | `3.15245e-8` m^2/s |
| `D_classical = G m/c` | `~ m^+1` | `2.02803e-49` m^2/s | `3.72377e-46` m^2/s |
| ratio | | `2.85e44` | `8.47e37` |

Electron -> proton (mass ratio 1836.15): `D_quantum` **decreases** by
`5.446e-4`; `D_classical` **increases** by `1836.15`. **The mass dependence is
inverted**, so no dimensionless constant can reconcile them, and the
magnitudes are apart by ~`10^44` besides.

Hence (iv) requires importing a constant of action dimension. If the substrate
supplies `hbar`, the postulate is **relocated, not removed** — and that must be
stated as such, not described as a derivation. This is structurally the same
failure mode as the alpha-web (see `codex/` and the Lean rigidity remark):
values that are postulates presented as substrate consequences.

## Recommendation

If gap 2 goes on the board, scope it as **(i)-(iii) only, with `D` a free
parameter**, and state up front that (iv) is an independent input. That is a
real and attackable problem — a path-space variational principle forcing
diffusivity conformal to the metric with constant factor — and it would be
genuine progress even with `D` unfixed.

Do **not** scope it as "derive `hbar/2m` from geometry". The mass-scaling
obstruction above is decisive, cheap to check, and would otherwise be
rediscovered after expensive work.
